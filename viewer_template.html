Calculation method · embedded configuration

From tire patch to chassis hardpoint

The model follows the load path in the same order the hardware carries it. A six-member rigid-upright solve produces axial linkage forces; the push/pull rod then drives a rigid rocker, the rocker drives the shock/spring assembly, and equal-and-opposite reactions are collected at the chassis.

These controls also set the 3D viewer. Diagrams use the active geometry and the default 3D orientation in an orthographic projection.

02External load

Reduce the tire load to one wrench

The tire force is transferred to the selected upright reference point. Its moment is the cross product of the offset from that reference and the applied force, plus any directly supplied moment.

W = [ F ; M ]
M = (rtire − rref) × F + Minput

Changing the reference point changes the individual moment numbers but not the physical member-force solution, provided every member moment arm is formed about the same reference.

03Geometry

Represent six ideal axial members

The upright is connected by exactly six independent force directions: tie/toe rod, lower fore, lower aft, upper fore, upper aft, and pushrod or pullrod. Each is idealized as pin-jointed and carries no bending moment.

ûi = (ranchor,i − rapplication,i) / ‖ranchor,i − rapplication,i‖
Fi = Ni ûi

With the direction defined from upright to chassis, positive N is tension and negative N is compression. Reversing only the stored member direction reverses the scalar sign but leaves the physical vector unchanged.

Model boundary: each fore and aft wishbone leg is treated as its own two-force member. Local A-arm bending, tabs, bolts, rod-end misalignment, compliance, fatigue, and joint friction require separate checks.

05Motion ratio

Balance the rocker about its measured axis

The solved push/pull-rod force is applied to the rocker. The shock force is found by balancing only the moment component about the rocker’s rotation axis. The shock chassis pickup remains fixed; only its rocker pickup rotates.

â · [(rrod − rpivot) × Frod + (rshock − rpivot) × Nshockûshock] = 0

Nshock = − momentrod,axis / momentshock,axis per lbf

The pivot reaction closes force equilibrium on the rocker. Reaction moment components not carried by the ideal rotation-axis torque balance are also reported for chassis review.

06Nonlinear pose

Iterate geometry against spring force

Ride-height coordinates are the reference pose. The model preserves the six linkage lengths while allowing three upright translations, three upright rotations, and one rocker rotation. A seventh residual matches calculated shock compression force to spring force.

R = [ ΔL1 … ΔL6 ; (−Nshock − Fride − k Δs) / k ] = 0

A finite-difference Newton iteration solves the seven constraints. Ride-height preload values are read from the embedded configuration.

07Output

Apply equal-and-opposite loads to the chassis

At every direct link anchor, the chassis load is equal and opposite to the member force acting on the suspension. The shock load enters at its fixed chassis pickup. The push/pull rod is internal to the suspension-plus-rocker free body, so it is not counted again as a chassis interface.

  • Wishbone and tie/toe-rod anchors receive axial link reactions.
  • The shock chassis pickup receives the axial shock reaction.
  • The rocker bearing receives an equivalent force and moment at the physical pivot axis.
  • The maximum-envelope view selects the largest |F| independently at each hardpoint.
